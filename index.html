The zero is not a zero

An audit of boar — one month, 387 experiments, a 1.7B model in a 3 GB APK with no internet permission. Everything below is recomputed from the author's published artefacts or measured in a run of my own; tags say which.

The finding, in one sentence: the headline "0 of 71 wins" is an artefact of taking one sample per item — the single item that was not a loss in the published Codex-family run is a stable win for the phone app, 3 out of 3, under the same judge model, when the judge is allowed to sample.

That does not rescue the app: the aggregate gap to the 0.50 bar is 5–10× larger than any judge noise I could measure, and it survives everything in this audit. It does mean the study is presenting a statistic with no resolution as its most quoted number, while the honest version — "at most about 4% of items win" — is both weaker and more defensible.

  • The floor is where the claim is made. Both families agree the app loses the other 70; nobody disputes that. What is disputed is the digit "0".
  • Rule of three on 0/71 gives a 95% upper bound of 4.1% — the ruler cannot distinguish zero wins from one, two, or three, and my measurement puts one on the board.

3. Why the win-rate was always going to read as zero measured + code

A win requires the judge to prefer the app in both presentation orders. The reference it has to beat scores an absolute 3.874 / 5 on the same rubric (abs_ref, G1 aggregates) and is written by the same model family that judges it. Under that ceiling the win condition is close to structurally unattainable: on the earlier v0.2 ruler, every configuration — including the desktop deep+thinking tier that is an order of magnitude larger — also scored exactly 0 wins. A metric that returns zero for a 35B model and for a 1.7B model is not measuring the 1.7B.

What I would publish instead. Lead with U and its interval: 0.167 [0.129–0.209], gap to the bar 0.33 — eight times the CI width, five to ten times the judge noise I measured. Then state the win-rate honestly as "≤ 4% of 71 items (95% bound); 1 item wins stably under re-judging at temperature 1.0". Same conclusion, no free ammunition for anyone who wants to dismiss the record.

4. Three more things I found while looking measured

a. The screen stage runs at α ≈ 0.25, and ~49 false positives were expected

387 registry rows resolve to about 250 independent hypotheses; 194 of them are judged screens. The frozen promotion rule (wins ≥ control+3 on 20-item slices, or Δ ≥ +0.03 at SE 0.045) implies a per-test α of about 0.25 — I checked both arms of it (P(wins≥12 | n=20, null) = 0.2517). The escalation ladder catches most of them: SEFIX-ALL gained +0.023 on the screen and was withdrawn when cross-validation gave −0.002. But of the 52 positive rows carrying a CI, Benjamini–Hochberg at q=0.05 leaves 19, and 4 of the 9 per-pack deltas (travel, health, outdoors, firstaid) do not survive a Bonferroni at k=9. The pooled Δ does not depend on them; the prose about individual packs does.

b. The "conditional prove" pack list contradicts the frozen criterion

PACKPROOF's pre-registered criterion reads sentences must use only packs that PROVE; the count was 0 of 9. The close-out report then introduces a weaker "conditional prove" rule and names 4 packs as satisfying it — a rule that appears in no criterion file — while one of those four (qa-health-se) has a pooled Δ of +0.05 with a CI of −0.15 to +0.25. That is the one place in the record where a number outruns its own rule.

c. "215 experiments without gain" is not 215 negative results

The registry splits it as 187 closed + 28 "not detected", and the registry's own definition says not-detected is not evidence of no effect; a parity arm is excluded from the count entirely. The honest phrasing is "about 250 hypotheses tested; 187 closed by their own frozen rule" — which is a stronger statement about method and a weaker one about the null.

5. The experiment this record should run next recommendation

The 4B model was killed on memory, not on quality — and the memory problem looks like a repack artefact rather than a phone limit:

  • At identical pack contexts the 4B beats the 1.7B by +0.121 (0.650 vs 0.529 on bench v0.1 EN, F-1401). That is the largest measured quality delta anywhere in the registry.
  • Its kill cause is recorded as ~2.3 GiB copied into anonymous memory by the ARM weight repack, RSS 4.7–5.0 GiB at kill (F-686) — while the same class of model on the same phone later completed 73/73 runs at PSS 4.76–4.92 GB (F-2078).
  • Quantisation was only ever tested upward (Q8_0 vs Q4_K_M, D-186). There is no 3-bit cell anywhere in the registry.

Run: Qwen3-4B at Q3_K_S or IQ3_XXS, q8_0 KV, mmap-backed weights with no anonymous repack, ctx 2048, two seeds. GO iff PSS ≤ 4.7 GB, zero LMK kills, bench ratio ≥ 0.60, p50 ≤ 40 s. Falsifier: ratio < 0.56 or PSS still over budget with mmap — then the drop was correct and this audit is wrong about it. Expected: bench +0.08 to +0.12, ruler U plausibly 0.165 → 0.25–0.35.
